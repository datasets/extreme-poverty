## discord

**New dataset: extreme poverty, world, regions and countries (World Bank PIP)**

In 2024, 824,298,368 people (10.1% of the world) lived on less than $3.00 a day, the World Bank's international poverty line in 2021 PPP dollars. In 1990 it was 2,300,636,087.

The fall happened almost everywhere except Sub-Saharan Africa. Outside the region the number of people in extreme poverty fell 87.7%, from 1,980,017,292 to 243,670,307. Inside it, the number rose 81.1%, from 320,618,795 to 580,628,061, even though the share of the region's people below the line fell from 61.5% to 45.0%. Sub-Saharan Africa went from 13.9% of the world's extremely poor to 70.4%.

The dataset has world and regional series for 1981 to 2026 at the $3.00, $4.20 and $8.30 lines (2025 and 2026 are nowcasts), and country estimates for every household survey year (172 economies). It is a frozen snapshot of the September 2026 release, CC BY 4.0.

I work on DataHub. This is the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform (PIP), September 2026 release, pulled from its API and reshaped into two long CSVs: world and regional poverty by year at the $3.00, $4.20 and $8.30 a day lines (2021 PPP), and country estimates for every survey year (2,588 survey estimates, 172 economies). CC BY 4.0, frozen snapshot, raw API files and build script in the repo.

One thing that stood out: in 1990, 320,618,795 of the 2,300,636,087 people below $3.00 a day (13.9%) lived in Sub-Saharan Africa. In 2024 it was 580,628,061 of 824,298,368 (70.4%). The region's poverty rate fell (61.5% to 45.0%), but its population grew faster.

Caveats: 2025 and 2026 are nowcasts. Regional figures for some years are flagged as projections because survey coverage is thin (for example South Asia 2015 to 2019). The June 2025 move to 2021 PPPs and the $3.00 line raised measured poverty, so these are not comparable with older $2.15 or $1.90 figures. Income and consumption surveys are not directly comparable. Happy to answer questions on method.
